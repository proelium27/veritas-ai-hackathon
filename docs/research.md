## 4. Rules, FAQ and judging (official)

**Rules** (<https://highschoolhackathon.org/about>, <https://highschoolhackathon.org/faq>)

- All project code must be written during the 48 hours. Learning, practicing and forming a team beforehand is fine.
- Public datasets, open-source libraries and pre-trained models are allowed if credited.
- AI tools like ChatGPT or Claude are allowed "to debug, explain concepts or speed up routine code". You must understand and be able to explain everything. "Finalists will be asked about their choices live."
- Teams of 1 to 3, from any school or country.
- Late submissions are not accepted. No exceptions for technical issues in the last minutes. They recommend submitting at least an hour early.
- Deliverables: working code (Colab notebook or GitHub repo they can open and run), a one-page write-up ("the problem, your approach, and how well it worked"), a 2-minute demo video (unlisted YouTube link or direct upload).

**Round 1 rubric (100 points)**, from <https://highschoolhackathon.org/judges>:

| Criterion | Points | Official wording |
|---|---|---|
| Technical execution | 30 | does it work, and is it well built? |
| Originality | 20 | is this a fresh take on the problem? |
| Impact and track fit | 20 | does it solve a real problem in the track? |
| Testing and honesty | 15 | did you measure how well it works, and do you know where it fails? |
| Communication | 15 | are the write-up and video clear? |

Round 1 is scored by the Veritas AI team, blind: "Reviewers don't see your name or school." Final ranking is Finals 70% + Round 1 30%.

**Finals rubric (100 points)**, same page, scored by a new panel:

| Criterion | Points |
|---|---|
| Demo and clarity | 30 |
| Technical depth | 30 |
| Impact | 20 |
| Q&A | 20 |

Finals format: 5-minute live demo on a public livestream, then questions from the panel (<https://highschoolhackathon.org/about>).

**Judges listed so far** (<https://highschoolhackathon.org/judges>): Kareema Batool (Researcher, Harvard Engineering and Applied Sciences / Microsoft), Niranjani Prasad (Senior Researcher, Microsoft Research Cambridge; Princeton PhD), Abdulla Kerimov (Stanford PhD, computational rock physics). Three more slots say "Announcing soon".

**Prizes** (<https://highschoolhackathon.org/prizes>): pool "over $75,000", 9 winning teams.

- Gold (1 team): remote internship at Arecibo AI for every member, plus project featured on veritasai.com.
- Silver (2 teams): 100% scholarship to Veritas AI Scholars, or 75% to the Veritas AI Fellowship.
- Bronze (3 teams): 50% scholarship to the Fellowship.
- Category awards: Best Beginner Build (100% Scholars scholarship), Best AI for Good (50% Fellowship), Judges' Choice (50% Fellowship).
- Everyone who submits gets a digital participation certificate.
- Prizes are non-transferable and have no cash value.

**Not affiliated with Harvard:** run by Veritas AI, founded by Harvard graduate students and alumni (<https://highschoolhackathon.org/faq>).

## Implications for us

- **Submit by about 10:59 PM Sun, and know where first (Technical execution, 30).** The site stresses firm deadlines and no tech-issue exceptions, and the submission method is still unknown. Read the starter kit email tonight and write the portal into `docs/competition.md` before kickoff.
- **Testing and honesty is worded the way our plan already treats it (15).** The official line is "did you measure how well it works, and do you know where it fails?" Keep the baseline comparison and the 3 to 5 failure examples as a fixed section in the notebook and a line in the write-up.
- **Originality and Impact (20 + 20) are scored "in the track".** The brief arrives at midnight with the track list. Pick the track in the first hours and tie the write-up to a named group of people. The Best AI for Good and Judges' Choice awards reward the same thing.
- **Round 1 is blind (Communication, 15).** Keep our names and school out of the write-up, video and notebook text where we can. The one-page write-up must carry the problem, approach and results on its own, since reviewers have no one to ask. The write-up follows the site's own wording: "the problem, your approach, and how well it worked".
- **Finals are 70% of the ranking, and Q&A and technical depth are 50 of 100 finals points.** All three of us must explain every part. The organizers say finalists will be questioned on their choices live, and AI help is allowed only if we can explain it. Start a short "why we chose this" list in `docs/log.md` as we build.
- **Beginner-friendly angle:** a Best Beginner Build award exists, and the starter notebooks are built for first-time builders. A simple, well-measured model beats an ambitious one that does not run top to bottom in a fresh Colab.
